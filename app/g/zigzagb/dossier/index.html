The story

Zig Zag is a 1982 unlicensed reworking of Namco's Dig Dug, credited on screen to LAX. This set is not merely a renamed Dig Dug ROM: it was rewritten for a modified Moon Cresta/Galaxian-style board with one Z80 and one AY-8910. The rules changed with the hardware. The pump is gone, enemies are defeated at close range, and a temporary pickaxe power-up speeds digging. Community catalogs also record the missing vegetable bonuses and a round-99 overflow or kill screen. The result is recognisably inspired by Dig Dug but behaves like its own compact conversion.

HARDWARE AND COPY PROTECTION

MAME models two 4 KiB program windows. Zig Zag can exchange the second and third program ROMs while running; the MAME driver explicitly describes this as copy protection. It is bank switching, not encrypted Z80 opcodes. The graphics packaging is unusual too. Each physical graphics ROM contains two halves. MAME selects one half for characters and continues into the other half for sprites, then uses the Pac-Man-bootleg graphics decode rather than the base Galaxian table. The board also has two sprite generators.

SURVIVING HARDWARE AND ART

Zig Zag appears to have circulated as a pirate conversion kit rather than a standardised dedicated cabinet. That is why no verified factory flyer or cabinet design is known. Surviving cabinets should be described as conversions, not as proof of an original production cabinet. A surviving instruction card is preserved in the official MAME artwork archive. The archive credits TheGuru for scanning the card and Mr. Do for cleaning it and producing the MAME layout. A documented PCB repair found a board whose video side largely follows Galaxian while the other half is substantially redesigned. The repairer found no complete schematic set, which helps explain why preservation depends on MAME source, surviving boards, photographs and community catalogs.
